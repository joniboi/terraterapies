# 1. The Cruip template explains exactly what we're seeing

Now that you've told me it comes from the free Simple template, I understand the "IKEA furniture" feeling you described. 😂

The problem isn't Cruip. It's that **your business is much richer visually than the template's default composition**.

On mobile, the problem is particularly obvious:

- enormous hero vertical space
- title and buttons floating in a lot of empty area
- then another large empty transition
- category card
- enormous gaps between sections
- footer with very little visual material

The mobile version isn't _bad_. It's clean and technically coherent.

But it feels like:

> **a SaaS landing page that has been dressed as a spa**

rather than:

> **a beautiful spa that happens to have a website.**

That's exactly what I'd change.

And I think your "carved artesan Renaissance artwork" analogy is actually very good. 😄

The website needs **art direction**, not more components.

I would tell the photographer **not just "take beautiful photos."**

You need photographs designed for **two different compositions**.

I'd aim for approximately **10–12 good landscape photographs**.

But not 12 variations of the same massage bed.

I'd divide them like this:

These are the most important.

# 7. Now, about the database: YES — I think this is worth fixing

And this is where I think your instinct is correct.

Your current structure:

```text
SERVICE GROUP
    ↓
CATEGORY
    ↓
TREATMENT
    ↓
VARIANT
```

is perfectly reasonable technically.

The problem is **the semantic meaning you've given to Group and Category**.

Right now you have something like:

```text
Treatments
 ├── Orientales
 ├── Relajantes
 ├── Reafirmantes
 ├── Terapéutico relajante
 ├── Faciales
 └── Bonos

Rituals
 ├── Rituales
 └── Ritual en Pareja
```

This isn't wrong.

But it doesn't represent the actual business architecture very well.

# 9. And there's an important technical consequence

You actually **don't necessarily need to change your database schema**.

Your existing schema can support this beautifully.

You could simply redefine `serviceGroups` as:

```text
Massages
Facials
Rituals
Couples
Packages
```

and then categories underneath them.

For example:

```text
serviceGroups

MASAJES
FACIALES
RITUALES
PAREJAS
BONOS
```

Then:

```text
categories

Orientales       → MASAJES
Relajantes       → MASAJES
Terapéuticos     → MASAJES
Reafirmantes     → MASAJES

Faciales         → FACIALES

Rituales         → RITUALES

Parejas          → PAREJAS

Bonos            → BONOS
```

And treatments stay exactly where they belong.

### You keep:

```text
Group
   ↓
Category
   ↓
Treatment
   ↓
Variant
```

No new database level required.

That is **much cleaner than adding `parentCategoryId`** just to make "Masajes" sit above Orientales.

# 11. One thing I'd change in naming

I'd probably rename:

> `serviceGroups`

to conceptually mean **"main service areas"**.

And I'd probably change:

> `Treatments`

from the current group name to something like:

> **Masajes**

because that's what it actually contains.

Your current use of "Treatments" as a group is too generic.

Likewise, `"Rituals"` is fine, but then `Ritual en Pareja` doesn't naturally belong exclusively there because **Masaje en Pareja** is also a legitimate treatment.

That's why making **Parejas its own group** is cleaner.
